## Winter Days

After an autumn that felt unusually short, the typical Berlin winter scenery has already arrived here and there. As every year, when I suddenly notice the grumpy-looking faces of Berliners (just to be clear, not a winter-only feature) and look around, the cloudy, gloomy, and dark winter days are in full swing without fail.

In terms of temperature, German winters are mostly similar to Korean ones, but the amount of sunlight is ridiculously low. The sun sets around three or four in the afternoon, and even before that, it hides behind the thick clouds that show up every single day. So you barely get to see the sun, and you only roughly figure it must be daytime because it's brighter than night. That's why I can't say I don't understand why people here complain about everything.

## Christmas Goose (Weihnachtsgans)

From what I've felt so far, German food culture leans heavily on nostalgia. When a certain season comes around or a holiday approaches, I often hear about dishes people make almost like a ritual, or stories of childhood memories related to them.

When winter fully sets in and Christmas approaches, Germans eat goose. Maybe it was a way to light up the house amid the long, long darkness all around and to feel the liveliness that comes with all the bustle. Goose was unfamiliar to me since it's an ingredient rarely used in Korea, and for someone like me who doesn't share the nostalgia Germans have, it tasted just so-so, like most other German dishes.
