I finally received my student residence permit[^1]. I had been preparing for it even before admission, but I was only able to wrap up the whole process after finishing my first semester. I'm leaving this short note here to record my trial and error, along with some new things I learned and felt throughout this exhausting journey.

## Visum vs. Aufenthaltserlaubnis

According to the Embassy of the Federal Republic of Germany in Seoul, "If you plan to stay for more than 90 days or if your purpose of stay is not tourism or a business trip, you must apply for a visa."[^2] However, thanks to the visa agreement between South Korea and Germany (effective Jan 1, 2005), Korean nationals can "also apply for visa issuance at the local immigration office within 90 days of entering Germany."[^3] That being said, local immigration offices in Germany issue a residence permit (Aufenthaltserlaubnis) rather than a visa (Visum), so the document you get locally seems to mean a residence permit. In my case, I already had a visa issued by the German Embassy in Seoul before entering the country for the first time, and I needed to change my residence status for my studies.

## Berlin

### Online Application (2025-09-29)

You can fill out the application form and upload all the necessary documents right on the [Landesamt für Einwanderung](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/) ([Berlin Immigration Office](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/en/)) website. No need to print out papers and carry them around in a binder! The submitted documents were as follows:[^4]

1. Application form (Auto-generated after filling out the online form)
2. Proof of health insurance
3. Passport
4. Currently valid visa or residence permit
5. Registration of address (Anmeldung)
6. Blocked account (Sperrkonto)
   1. Account opening confirmation
   2. Bank balance certificate
7. Letter of admission (Zulassungsbescheid)

At the final step, I paid the 100-euro fee by card. I was quite amazed that this entire process was possible without an appointment (Termin), paper documents, or cash.

### Fiktionsbescheinigung (2025-10-8)

Just two days later, I received an email from the Berlin Immigration Office with a scheduled date and time for my visit. It included instructions to come and pick up a temporary residence permit. On the spot, they checked my passport and visa, I paid the 13-euro issuance fee (card only), and I received it right away. The duration was a generous 6 months, giving me plenty of time to go through the next steps. 

Although there isn't much you can actually do with it, a temporary residence permit (Fiktionsbescheinigung) inherits the exact conditions of your existing visa or residence permit. If you originally entered visa-free for 90 days and fail to get your desired residence permit quickly, you might face work restrictions or, more importantly, a situation where you leave Germany (outside the German border, not just the EU!) and cannot re-enter. You need to check if you fall under the condition of **Der Aufenthaltstitel als fortbestehend (§ 81 Abs. 4 AufenthG)**.

Just to be sure, I asked the clerk about the status of my student residence permit application and got an unexpected answer: the process had been paused. Before I asked, absolutely no one had formally notified me of this crucial fact (and still hasn't..). The reason was that the Berlin Immigration Office can only issue student residence permits to students residing within Berlin and Brandenburg. They told me there was no need to cancel the application itself; once I registered my address in Aachen, my case would automatically be transferred to the local immigration office there, and I could just pick up where I left off. Since I hadn't even found an apartment in Aachen yet and my temporary permit still had plenty of time left, I decided to just forget about this whole process for a while.

## Aachen

### Immigration Office Registration

I finally signed a housing contract and swiftly completed my address registration (Anmeldung) on the very same day. But when I tried to book an appointment with the Aachen Immigration Office, it didn't work. I went to the office one morning when I had some free time, waited in line, and was told that I had to register my information into their system first. This was a step that didn't exist in Berlin. Fortunately, I brought my passport, residence permit, and Anmeldung, so I applied for registration right there. However, it takes 1-2 weeks for the registration to go through, during which you can't do anything at all, not even book an appointment.

### Booking an Appointment (2025-12-11)

They didn't send any separate notification regarding the registration result. I just checked the website one day, saw that the system allowed me to book an appointment, and scheduled it for an available day. Of course, the earliest available date was 2 months later...

### Student Residence Permit Application (2026-02-25)

I finally finished applying for the student residence permit. Honestly, after dealing with the aforementioned trial and error, side tasks, and waiting for the appointment, I was only just beginning the very first step toward acquiring the actual permit. Nevertheless, perhaps because there was a record of my application being submitted in Berlin and transferred over to Aachen, I was able to get an interim certificate approving my residence permit right on the spot (Vorläufige Bescheinigung gem. § 3 7 Abs.2 VwVfG NRW über einen bewilligten Aufenthaltstitel).

On this day, I printed out and brought all the previously submitted documents along with some updated ones.

1. Application form (Aachen Immigration Office PDF form[^5])
2. Proof of health insurance
3. Passport (I had my passport reissued in the meantime...)
   1. Expired passport
   2. Currently valid passport
4. Currently valid visa or residence permit
   1. Previous visa
   2. Temporary residence permit (Fiktionsbescheinigung)
5. Registration of address (Anmeldung)
6. Blocked account (Sperrkonto)
   1. Account opening confirmation
   2. Bank balance certificate (most recent issue date)
7. Certificate of enrollment
   1. Certificate of study (Immatrikulationsbescheinigung)
8. Passport photo

I didn't have to pay the application fee since I had already paid it in Berlin.

Since I got a new passport, I was worried about my passport details being different from my initial application. I even went out of my way to prepare my passport issuance history in English from Government24 with an online Apostille certificate, but they handled it with just a simple visual check.

The rule for passport photos had changed; you must now submit photos taken either at the on-site kiosk or at a photo studio that provides a QR code. The notice I read said to bring a biometric photo, so I was caught off guard and unintentionally ended up getting my picture taken on the spot looking like a mugshot...

You can check the issuance status online, and a little less than 2 weeks later, the status changed to ready for pickup. The clerk had told me it usually takes 2 to 4 weeks. You can apply for an express service for an additional fee. From what I heard from people around me, they managed to get it in 5 days with that option.

### Picking up the Student Residence Permit (2026-04-13)

Even picking it up requires an appointment... I thought it was all over, but the final hurdle was waiting. The next available date popped up as 3 weeks away. I spent a few days refreshing the page whenever I thought about it, and luckily, an early morning slot on a closer date opened up, so I managed to squeeze it into my schedule.

I thought I would just be picking it up, but it took some time because I had to sit down with a staff member at the desk for identity verification and the cancellation of my old residence permit.

Then I came home, set up my BundID, and finally, this whole process was over.
